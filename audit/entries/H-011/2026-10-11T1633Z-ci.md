## What the entry claims

The owner invests 500 euros a month across three funds — seventy percent world stocks, twenty percent euro government bonds, ten percent gold. The rule sends each month's money to whichever funds have fallen below their target share, rather than splitting every payment seventy/twenty/ten. On forty-five years of market history ending in 2017 (the practice period, not the sealed one), this kept the account's mix about three times closer to target than plain splitting — the test requires twice as close — while costing about an eighth of a percentage point a year against holding the same average amount of stock, inside the quarter-point limit. The control did not wear out in the later years of the ten-year savings plans tested. The rule never sells anything and never bets on which asset will rise.
